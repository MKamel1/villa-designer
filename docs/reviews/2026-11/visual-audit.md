## Distribution and QA blindness

- A 0; B 1 (v34); B- 4; C+ 3; C 7; C- 5; D+ 9; D 6; F 3. The median grade is C-/D+.
- QA passed 35 of 38 views. I grade 27 of those 35 at C or below, so 77 % of QA passes are false passes. Every QA FAIL (v15, v29, v36) is a correct fail. QA is only sensitive to gross luminance and colour statistics.
- The worst false pass is v30, essentially an F, which passed with two warnings. Five more false passes are D: v03, v08, v26, v27, v37.
- No QA check fires on geometry crudeness, materials, occluders, frame-edge artifacts (v19 black band, v27 seam), intersections (v39) or emptiness. WARN never escalates, even when there are three.

## Five systemic defect classes

1. **Low-poly and procedural assets**: faceted globe pendants and wall lights (v01, v20, v22); 12-sided pots (v25–v28); card-leaf, fishbone and lollipop plants (v26, v27, v28, v36, v37, v40); box bunk, headboards and poufs (v06, v13, v02/v10/v24); board-like clothes (v31, v32); cushion-through-cage (v39). *Suspected cause: asset quality.* Procedural generators and cheap packs, with no polycount, silhouette or interpenetration gate. The garden stock is the weakest; the frangipani and rattan chairs show what a real asset does.
2. **Exposure and dynamic range under the set-wide exposure lock**: crushed views (v03, v29, v30, v36, v13, v35) and clipped windows and exteriors (v04, v06, v15, v22, v23). darkviews-report.md confirms the lock is deliberate (the day cohort gain on v29 fell 4.1 stops). Windows open onto untextured white massing. *Cause: lighting and exposure policy*, plus no window/interior balance (no fill or local tone mapping) and no context assets. Fidelity rules forbid per-view boosting, so the fix is a presentation development or exemption decided by the lead or client. Leaving the images unreadable is not a fix.
3. **Material scale, mapping and colour**: marble over-scaled with blotchy rust veins in every wet room; wood grain about 3–5x too large and salmon-tinted (v09, v31, v35 door); stretched or diagonal UVs (v11 treads, v18 sideboard, v32); casts (blue v15, cyan v37/v39, green coves v17/v24). *Cause: materials.* Texture world-scale is not tied to physical tile and veneer size, and there is no per-material albedo or hue sanity check.
4. **Camera and composition**: foreground occluders (curtain v24, wall v16/v23, door v35, panel v34, counter v28); frame-edge artifacts (black band v19, hard seam v27, haze glass v37); 16 mm used in 8 views, which stretches the corners against the owner's 24 mm benchmark; cropped subjects (v01 pendant, v16 basin, v22 headboard); views of sofa backs (v04, v18); bent wall/ceiling lines (v08, v30). *Cause: camera placement* that verifies only that the subject's bounding box is in frame.
5. **Styling and emptiness**: one sofa with the same zig-zag cushions is cloned into 7 views; books are uniform blocks; WCs and baths are empty; the "cinema" has no screen or cinema seats; kids' rooms have no personality; walls are blank (v17, v40). *Cause: styling.* No dressing layer and no repetition check across the set.

## Rubric for an automated vision critic

The critic should score each item 0–2 with evidence and hard-fail any item scored 0 on a hero view.

1. Asset fidelity: visible faceting on curved objects (globes, pots, cushions); flat-card or disc foliage; box-primitive furniture, bedding or clothing.
2. Contact and intersection: objects floating (lamps, nightstands); objects penetrating (cushion through cage, pillow into headboard); no contact shadow.
3. Readable range: share of the subject region that is crushed or clipped, measured on the named subject's pixels, not the whole frame. Windows must show a legible exterior, not white.
4. Context: exterior and neighbour surfaces untextured or flat white; blank sky slabs.
5. Material plausibility: texture feature scale against known physical size (marble vein spacing, wood grain width, paver size); stretched or smeared UVs; unnatural hue on wood and stone.
6. Colour cast: per-view mid-tone cast, including cyan and green, with a stricter cool limit than now.
7. Frame integrity: black or solid bands at the frame edges; hard straight discontinuities through objects; haze or veils from a pane in front of the camera.
8. Occlusion and composition: a near occluder covering more than 15 % of the frame; the subject cropped at the frame edge; the camera behind furniture backs; empty wall covering more than 40 %.
9. Lens and verticals: lens under 20 mm flagged unless justified; bent straight edges; keystoning.
10. Subject truth: the view name matches what is shown (cinema has a screen and seating; dressing has clothes that read as garments).
11. Styling density and repetition: props per square metre of visible floor; identical hero furniture or cushions repeated across more than 3 views.
12. Benchmark parity: a pairwise "which looks more like a photograph" judgment against bedroom-overcast-door.png. Fail if the view loses clearly.

The critic should also escalate when a view has 2 or more WARNs, or when it fails any rubric item, rather than letting the view pass.
