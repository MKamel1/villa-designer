# 0 — Intent

This is a working stage package. Methods and examples are project-authored; published support is limited to the evidence cards linked below.

## Inputs and interview

Required: household, routines, hosting, privacy, accessibility, maintenance, budget, taste. Each fact records value, status and provenance; unknown is not an assumed pass.

- Who is awake earliest, and who needs quiet then?
- What do you enjoy in a hotel, and what would be inconvenient at home?
- Which three outcomes should survive a budget reduction?

## Method

1. Translate each routine into a requirement with an identifier, priority and observable test.
2. Read references for proportion, palette, texture, lighting and atmosphere; obtain client response to a small illustrated direction.
3. Record disagreement, maintenance tolerance and budget trade-offs before asking for approval.

## Worked example

Two adults work at home; children sleep before weekly guests leave. Brief separates study calls and sleeping access from entertaining. Warm timber is a pilot preference, not a universal luxury requirement. See [pilot diagrams](../pilot-concepts.svg) and [bedroom exercise](../bedroom-worked-example.md).

## Checks and failures

- Measured geometry and source-validated targets where available; otherwise unresolved
- Failure: asking the client to choose duct sizes. Remedy: ask about noise, sleep and room use; develop technical implications in Systems.
- Failure: calling every large room luxurious. Remedy: test routines and prioritise useful space in Fit.

## Deliverables and approval

Deliver: quality_brief, design_direction.

- Routines and priorities are specific
- Taste references analysed into observable attributes
- Budget scope and exclusions understood

Record reviewer, current revision, outcome and reasons for every qualitative criterion. Present the recommendation, household reason, visual explanation, evidence and cost/space/maintenance consequences. Request the client’s explicit decision only on a concrete reviewed proposal. Unresolved evidence or missing facts keep the gate open.
