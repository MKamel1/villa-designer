# 1 — Ground

This is a working stage package. Methods and examples are project-authored; published support is limited to the evidence cards linked below.

## Inputs and interview

Required: location, climate, hemisphere, north, boundary, levels, access, neighbours, trees, utilities, views, noise, wind. Each fact records value, status and provenance; unknown is not an assumed pass.

- Where would you want to sit outside at breakfast?
- Which neighbouring views or noises bother you?

## Method

1. Inventory boundaries, existing buildings, trees, levels, access and utilities; attach provenance to each fact.
2. Draw true north, views, noise, seasonal shade and neighbours on one diagram. Test seasonal solar geometry using actual latitude.
3. Keep tree roots, drainage, equipment and garden paths in the same site strategy; do not substitute planting imagery for a maintainable landscape.

## Worked example

Pilot ground is a fictional 30 by 40 metre rectangle in a northern-hemisphere hot-dry scenario; road south and views north. These are known scenario inputs, not surveyed facts. Summer wind is an assumption needing weather evidence. See [pilot diagrams](../pilot-concepts.svg) and [bedroom exercise](../bedroom-worked-example.md).

## Checks and failures

- Measured geometry and source-validated targets where available; otherwise unresolved
- Failure: copying Australian north-facing heating advice. Remedy: recompute seasonal sun for the actual hemisphere; reopen Order and Systems.
- Failure: trees shown over a service trench. Remedy: coordinate root space and utility routes before room placement.

## Deliverables and approval

Deliver: site_constraints, landscape_strategy.

- Surveyed facts separated from assumptions
- Sun wind views and noise inform orientation
- Landscape water and access conflicts recorded

Record reviewer, current revision, outcome and reasons for every qualitative criterion. Present the recommendation, household reason, visual explanation, evidence and cost/space/maintenance consequences. Request the client’s explicit decision only on a concrete reviewed proposal. Unresolved evidence or missing facts keep the gate open.
