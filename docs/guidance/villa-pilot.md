# Fictional villa: early-design pilot

This is an authored scenario, not the user's site. Scenario inputs are known by
definition; no survey, local climate file or client approval is claimed. The
original placeholder brief and site remain untouched.

The household has two working adults and two children, hosts weekly, wants quiet
bedroom access, sheltered garden dining and calm tactile interiors. A warm timber
and mineral palette is a provisional taste direction. Routine: guests arrive,
coats and shoes are stored, entertaining opens to the garden, and children reach
bedrooms without crossing the gathering. Storage and service paths are part of
the plan, not leftover space.

The fictional plot is 30 metres east-to-west by 40 metres north-to-south. True
north is at the top of the [diagrams](pilot-concepts.svg). The scenario assumes a
hot-dry climate, a southern road, northern garden views, an eastern neighbour,
level terrain and a western service approach. Trees and utilities are not
surveyed; planting and drainage routes remain provisional. Latitude 30 degrees
north is a study input only. Orientation needs seasonal sun and wind verification;
Australian directional recommendations cannot simply be transplanted.
See [Your Home: Orientation](https://www.yourhome.gov.au/passive-design/orientation).

## Feasibility

The room schedule in the manifest totals 272 square metres of usable area.
Adding 40 for circulation, 32 for walls, 12 for structural zones and 14 for
services gives 370 square metres. The scenario allowance is 400 square metres,
leaving 30 square metres before level-by-level refinement. The plot area is
1,200 square metres. These are explicit project inputs and arithmetic, not
published luxury minima or statutory permissions.

An initial allocation places living, dining, kitchen, guest bedroom, ground
bathroom, utility, study, storage and entry on the ground floor: 160 square metres
usable. The upper level has 112 square metres usable. Ground allowances of 24
circulation, 20 walls, 7 structure and 9 services produce a 220 square metre
footprint; upper allowances of 16, 12, 5 and 5 produce 150 square metres. Their
combined area reconciles to 370. Unbuilt plot area is not all garden: paths,
terraces, vehicle access, utilities and drainage also need space.

Cost is unresolved: no currency, location-specific rates, date, specification
level or budget has been supplied. A later estimate must separately show building,
landscape, furnishings, fees, taxes, contingency and exclusions. Compare initial
and maintenance costs, rather than claiming that bigger or more elaborate means
better. [Your Home: Affordability](https://www.yourhome.gov.au/buy-build-renovate/affordability).

## Three alternatives

| Concept | Arrival and organisation | Benefits | Costs and unresolved coordination |
|---|---|---|---|
| Garden bar | Southern arrival to hall, entertaining toward northern garden; private stair/hall bypasses living | Legible private route, one main garden, concentrated services | West study needs shade; road-noise buffer and garden-opening lintel need design |
| Courtyard | Arrival bends around a planted court; rooms face an inward outdoor room | Sheltered outdoor sequence and multiple garden edges | More external wall, corners, cleaning and waterproof junctions; overlooking and airflow unresolved |
| Pavilions | Entrance flows through glazed entertaining room to separate sleeping wing | Dramatic garden sequence and distinct masses | Private route crosses hosting; long glazed span unsupported; west glare and heat unresolved |

The diagrams are original spatial studies, not dimensioned architecture. Each
concept must later produce coordinated plans, sections, elevations, supports and
service routes; the drawing convention does not certify structure. The current
connectivity tests catch the pavilion's private-route failure even if a hero
render looks attractive. Structure and comfort remain explicitly unresolved.

## Recommendation

Develop the garden bar. It suits this family's bedtime and hosting routine while
preserving a continuous garden and compact service core. Organise the guest room
off the private hall; stack upper bathrooms over ground wet rooms; reserve
service distribution beside circulation. Show supports flanking the main opening
and size them through later professional coordination. Locate shaded outdoor
dining next to kitchen and dining, with a separate maintenance path and drainage.

The recommendation is our synthesis, supported by process and comfort principles,
not a published endorsement of this plan. The American Institute of Architects
describes schematic communication through plans, sections and elevations and
later structure/services coordination; it does not mandate this layout.
[Design-phase services](https://www.aia.org/resource-center/defining-the-architects-basic-services).
Norm Architects' arrival description suggests a restrained tactile threshold;
using that idea domestically requires adapting scale and maintenance.
[1 Hotel Copenhagen](https://normcph.com/project/1-hotel-copenhagen/).

Compared with the courtyard, the bar proposes fewer complex edges and a simpler
service arrangement, but no cost saving is quantified without quantities and
rates. A screen and deep reveal may support privacy and the intended atmosphere;
glare, thermal performance and acoustic isolation still need assessment. The
client decision would be whether this balance merits developed drawings. No
client decision is recorded, and the incomplete design is not approval-ready.

## Critical review prompts

Challenge the recommendation independently against arrival, service deliveries,
bedtime routes, west glare, road noise, structural depth, stair geometry, wet
stacking, accessible guest bathing, storage and landscape drainage. The existing
pilot graph establishes only connectivity. It cannot prove clear width, actual
sightlines, stair feasibility, airflow, cooling loads or construction details.
Those missing demonstrations must stay visible in the next presentation.

## Response to independent critique

The packaged revision is `pilot-2`; the independent critique records the earlier
`pilot-1` inputs and a follow-up on the corrections. No approval carries forward.

The [independent critic](pilot-independent-critique.md) found that the first
garden-bar diagram sent the arrival route through the guest block. The revised
diagram reserves a separate entry strip connected to the private hall. This
corrects the diagram/graph contradiction; it does not establish real clear widths
or a complete floor plan. The original critique is retained as the review record.

The manifest now marks unspecified facts as missing and provisional structure,
services, neighbour geometry and landscape strategies as assumed. It no longer
uses a generic narrative pointer to imply that these inputs are known.

Two adults working at home still have only one scheduled study. The brief must
resolve whether simultaneous calls require a second room or an adequately divided
study. A second work room would consume the current area reserve or force a
trade-off. Work privacy, stair fit, actual service routes, support sizes, glare,
acoustics and garden drainage remain open. The lead retains the garden bar as a
provisional direction, subject to those tests; no gate is approved.
