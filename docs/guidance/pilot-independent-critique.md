# Independent critique of the fictional villa pilot

Reviewed 2026-09-24 by a separately tasked architectural critic. Reviewed inputs:
[pilot narrative](villa-pilot.md), [concept diagrams](pilot-concepts.svg),
[project manifest](../../knowledge/projects/villa-pilot.json), revision `pilot-1`,
and all eight [stage packages](stages/0-intent.md).

The household, rectangular site, latitude, climate category and area allowances
are authored scenario inputs, not client statements or surveyed conditions.
This critique evaluates internal consistency and missing design demonstrations.
It does not establish technical dimensions, climate performance, engineering
adequacy or code compliance. No client gate is approved.

The garden bar is a reasonable candidate for further study, but its claimed
private route and compact services are not yet demonstrated by coordinated
drawings. Keep the recommendation provisional and retain the courtyard as a
live comparator until the following issues are resolved.

| Issue and observed evidence | Consequence and required response |
|---|---|
| **Arrival contradicts the privacy claim.** The garden-bar diagram's arrival line runs through the block labelled “Guest / private” before reaching the bypass hall. The manifest instead connects entry directly to hall. Neither establishes the upper-floor bedroom route. | Redraw a distinct entrance and stair connecting directly to the hall; locate the guest-room door. Test guest arrival and children's bedtime on both storeys, including sightlines and door swings. Do not report privacy as demonstrated while drawing and connectivity disagree. |
| **Area arithmetic is not geometric feasibility.** The stated room areas reconcile to 272 square metres; allowances produce 370, with ground and upper allocations of 220 and 150 respectively. No scaled footprint or stair section proves those allocations. Entry is already a usable room while circulation is an additional allowance, so boundaries need explicit definitions. | Produce both floor plans and an area overlay separating usable rooms, circulation, walls, structure and services without overlap. Allocate stair, landings and any void consistently; show the upper footprint over the ground floor. The 30-square-metre remaining allowance is not demonstrated spare capacity. |
| **The brief omits a work conflict and accessible use.** Two adults work at home, but only one study is scheduled; simultaneous calls are not tested. Accessibility and maintenance facts point to the narrative without defining user needs or tolerance. | Add an explicit fictional simultaneous-work scenario or record it as unresolved. Test the ground guest bedroom, bathroom, entrance and garden route against a defined household use case before selecting geometry. Keep maintenance and accessibility unresolved until there is an actual scenario answer. |
| **Structural and services compactness is asserted.** The garden lintel is flagged, but no support layout, upper-floor loading arrangement, stair opening, wet-room overlay or equipment zone is drawn. The connectivity record's service room is reached from entry and does not demonstrate the proposed western service approach. | Draw one coordinated section and overlays showing tentative support continuity, beam depth, stacked wet rooms, distribution space, equipment access and delivery route. Reconcile lost area or height with Fit and Rooms. Describe structural sizes and comfort performance as unresolved until checked by appropriate methods. |
| **Climate is a category, not a tested design response.** “Hot-dry” and latitude 30 degrees north do not establish seasonal wind, solar exposure, glare, night conditions or outdoor comfort. The western study and northern terrace have no shade geometry. | Use clearly labelled fictional weather assumptions for the demonstration, or obtain location-specific data for a real project. Study seasonal sun and occupied outdoor times; show shading, curtains and furniture together. Compare the courtyard's shelter with the bar under the same assumptions before claiming either performs better. |
| **Landscape space and privacy are unallocated.** The diagrams show green land but no vehicle area, terrace dimensions, maintenance path, drainage levels, planting root zones or equipment. Eastern-neighbour overlooking and southern-road noise are acknowledged but untested. | Produce a site overlay reserving these uses and draw the indoor-outdoor threshold in section. Show neighbouring sightlines, bedroom exposure and the service approach. Preserve unknown trees, utilities, wind and drainage as unresolved; generic narrative pointers are not evidence that these facts exist. |
| **Alternative comparison is uneven and proof is absent.** The pavilion is intentionally given a crossing route; a repaired pavilion with a private connection has not been costed in area. The courtyard and garden bar have identical simplified connectivity despite different spatial claims. Requirement artifact records are empty, with no plans, sections, elevations or reviewed rendering binding. | Give every alternative the same area definitions and household tests. Either sketch the pavilion repair and its consequences or explicitly bound its rejection to the version shown. Attach current artifacts to each priority requirement and record a response to this critique before asserting presentation readiness. |

The next review should receive coordinated two-storey sketches, one principal
section, a site and landscape overlay, comparable area schedules, and a short
requirement-to-drawing record. It should also receive a missing-input list that
distinguishes invented scenario assumptions from information not yet supplied.
No numerical design minimum is introduced by this critique.

Until then, the defensible client-facing recommendation is: **develop the garden
bar to test its promised private circulation and garden relationship**. Its
cost, technical feasibility, acoustic privacy and environmental comfort remain
unresolved, and the client has not accepted the proposal.

## Independent follow-up, 2026-09-24

Re-read the revised diagram, narrative, manifest and arrival regression. The
table above records the original review; the following findings supersede its
description of the corrected artifacts.

- **Diagram contradiction corrected:** the arrival now passes through a
  separately labelled entry strip to the hall before entering the guest/private
  block. Inspection of the drawing geometry confirms that its initial arrival
  segment avoids that block. The regression reads these actual drawing elements
  and also checks the original crossing geometry. This resolves the specific
  diagram/graph mismatch; it does not establish stair access, clear widths,
  sightlines or sound privacy.
- **Missing-input reporting corrected:** accessibility, maintenance, trees,
  utilities and wind are explicitly missing. Structure, services, landscape and
  neighbour geometry are recorded as assumptions with their limits. Their
  previous generic narrative pointers no longer imply completed knowledge.
- **Work conflict acknowledged:** the narrative and an unresolved decision now
  expose the two-worker/one-study issue and its area consequence. The reporting
  omission is resolved; the design choice remains open.

Scaled two-storey feasibility, structural and service overlays, climate studies,
landscape allocation, comparable alternatives and requirement-linked artifacts
remain outstanding. The manifest still names revision `pilot-1`; assign a new
revision when packaging the revised proposal so the original critique and its
response can be distinguished in later review records. The garden bar remains
a provisional development direction. No design gate is approved.
