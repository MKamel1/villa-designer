# A+ — what "done" means for the villa

This is the yardstick for all further work. It is written from the point of view of a top
professional practice completing the villa: what they would hand over, and the bar each item
must clear. The pipeline is graded against it; nothing is called finished until it passes here.

## 1. The professional test

The villa is A+ when all four of these are true:

1. **A licensed reviewer would return no compliance comments.** An Egyptian architect and electrical
   engineer could review, stamp and submit the permit set without rework. (The signature itself stays
   with them: Egyptian permits require a Syndicate-registered engineer, ER Art.112.)
2. **A contractor could price and build it.** Dimensioned drawings, schedules, specifications and
   costed products leave nothing to guess.
3. **The client recognises their home.** The brief (shade, colourful planting, frangipani, terracotta,
   a distinct identity for each daughter) is visibly delivered, and the client signs off each gate.
4. **The pictures could be photographs.** In a blind side-by-side test against real studio
   photographs, a viewer picks the render as CG no better than chance.

## 2. How criteria are written

- **Every criterion is checkable**: a script, a measurement, a reference calculation it must reproduce,
  or a named reviewer step with a 0/1/2 rubric.
- **Every threshold has a status.** It is either *sourced* (page read in a held book or standard),
  *STD* (standard known but page not yet carded), *POLICY* (our own pre-registered bar, never tuned to
  pass), or *UNVERIFIED* (must not gate anything until sourced). Egyptian law wins where it sets a
  value; a stricter international value is the A+ target on top of the legal gate.
- **Gates vs targets.** A *gate* is a legal or safety minimum; failing it caps the grade at D. A
  *target* is the A+ level above it.

## 3. Grade scale (applies to every discipline)

| Grade | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **A+** | Every gate and target passes with a source; the full deliverable set is issued and consistent; blind tests won. |
| **A** | All gates pass; ≤3 targets missed, each documented with a reason. Issue-ready. |
| **B** | All gates pass; deliverable set incomplete (e.g. no details, schedules not generated, exterior missing). |
| **C** | Readable and partly checked, but gates unverified or deliverables largely absent. Not submittable. |
| **D** | A safety or legal gate fails. Not permittable. |
| **F** | Absent or unreadable. |

**Overall grade = the lowest discipline grade**, plus the cross-discipline checks in §5. A beautiful
render cannot lift an unsafe stair.

## 4. Per discipline: the bar and where we stand

### Architecture — today D+ → A+
**Deliver:** site plan with planning envelope; GA plans for all three levels at 1:50 with grids,
levels and dimensions; four elevations; two sections (through the stair and through a sunken garden);
enlarged stair and wet rooms 1:20; wall sections and details; door, window, area and room-finish
schedules; outline then full specification; fire/escape strategy; daylight and shading report; the
permit set per ER Art.111–112, bilingual.

**Gates (Egyptian Executive Regulations of Law 119/2008; text to be checked against the Arabic Gazette):**
- Clear height ≥2.70 m in rooms (2.30 m halls/baths) — Art.93
- Habitable room ≥7.5 m², min 2.5 m — Art.94; doors ≥80 cm — Art.97
- Openings onto a road or courtyard ≥8 % of floor area for habitable rooms, **basement included** — Art.96
- Sunken gardens must qualify as courtyards: min side ≥max(0.25H, 3 m), area ≥max(9 m², (H/4)²) — Art.98
- Stairs: width ≥110 cm, tread ≥27 cm, riser ≤17 cm, ≤14 risers per flight, headroom ≥2.1 m — Art.100
- Railings ≥90 cm and stop objects ≥10 cm — Art.103 (A+ target: 1100 mm at drops to gardens, ADK)
- Fire-code exemption holds only if every storey ≤400 m², top floor ≤16 m, basement ≤3 m below street — Art.106

**Targets:** closed risers; escape route or escape window from every room; external shading on every
sun-facing habitable opening (client prefers shade); zero structure clashes; design rubric ≥16/20.

### Interior design — today C− → A+
**Deliver:** concept with named style, mood board and palette with LRVs; layouts with clearance
overlays; finish plans and finish schedule (every floor, wall, base and ceiling coded); interior
elevations of every joinery or tiled wall; millwork drawings; sample boards; FF&E schedule with SKU,
Egyptian supplier, EGP price, lead time, alternate and PO status; art and styling programme.

**Bar:** one concept traceable through every room; ≤3 woods/stones and ≤2 metals per room; no cloned
hero pieces; 100 % real products within the budget cap; kitchen aisle ≥1219 mm, walkway ≥914 mm
(NKBA); wardrobes 610 mm deep; wet floors R10/R11 (showers class B); bunk guard ≥160 mm above the
mattress; every living space and bedroom styled.

### Lighting — today C+ → A+
**Deliver:** design intent; luminaire schedule (lm, W, CCT, CRI/R9, SDCM, UGR, IP, driver, dimming,
flicker); dimensioned reflected ceiling plans; calculation report per room and scene; controls intent
with DALI scene table; exterior and obtrusive-light report; emergency and night-path layout; daylight
report; energy and maintenance.

**Bar:** every task within ±10 % of its target and **not over-lit** (≤1.5× in the design scene);
no bright source in the bed, sofa or dining sightlines; luminaires set out on axes; ≥2 switched layers
per room and named scenes; one colour temperature per layer; exterior lighting that keeps light on
neighbours ≤10 lx and upward light ≤0.15 (SLL zone E3); 1 lx emergency path on stairs and the basement
route; calculations reproduce published reference cases.

### Electrical — today F → A+
**Deliver:** design basis; small-power and lighting-circuit layouts; equipment and load schedules
with maximum demand; cable schedule; distribution-board schedules; single-line diagram; earthing,
bonding and lightning-risk assessment; low-voltage systems (data, Wi-Fi, CCTV, intercom, fire
detection); bathroom and outdoor special locations; Revit circuits read back.

**Bar:** carded device provision per room; no socket in bathroom zones 0–2; 30 mA RCD on sockets,
bathrooms and outdoors; cables sized for 45–50 °C roof runs with voltage drop ≤3 % lighting / ≤5 %
other; disconnection times met; phases balanced within 10 %; schedules, single-line diagram and Revit
agree to the watt. Governing code: the Egyptian electrical installations code (number and edition to be
obtained from HBRC); IEC 60364 until then.

### Renders — today median C−/D+ → A+
**Bar (R1–R13):** 24 mm lens by default (24–35 mm), camera 1.2–1.6 m, verticals within 0.5°; subject
≤1 % clipped and ≤2 % crushed; textured exterior and neighbours through windows; texture sharp at
viewing distance and at real-world scale (±20 %); no visible faceting, card foliage or box furniture;
nothing floating or intersecting; no occluder over 15 % of the frame; styled, uncloned rooms; wins or
ties a blind comparison with real photographs.

**Who judges:** an automated vision critic scores every view first, but it may only gate after it has
caught the 27 known bad views from the November audit (recall reported). The client is the final gate.

## 5. Cross-discipline checks (needed for overall A+)

- **One model, one truth.** Drawings, schedules, calculations and renders all come from the same design
  model; counts and positions agree (doors in schedule = doors on plan = doors in render).
- **Lighting ↔ electrical ↔ ceiling.** Every luminaire is on one circuit and one control zone; it sits
  on the reflected ceiling plan, in the ceiling void depth, and in the render at the same position.
- **Interior ↔ lighting.** Palette reflectances used in the lighting calculation match the finish schedule.
- **Architecture ↔ renders.** Every hero view shows what the plans say (no invented content, per the
  "renders must be faithful" rule).
- **Coherence review.** A reviewer finds no contradiction across the set in 15 minutes.

## 6. Open sourcing work (before these thresholds can gate)

- Check the Executive Regulations rows against the Arabic Gazette text (translation used: Andersen, unofficial).
- Obtain the Egyptian fire code, disability code and electrical installations code (number and edition from HBRC).
- Card the STD values from IEC 60364, EN 12464-1/-2, EN 1838, LM-83 pages.
- Read the NKBA bath clear-floor figure; find a tip-over anchoring standard.
- Agree with the client the POLICY bars that touch taste (props density, material counts).
